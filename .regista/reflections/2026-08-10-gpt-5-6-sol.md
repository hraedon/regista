## On the project

Regista is unusually disciplined about naming the limits of its evidence, and the 0.6.0 work is making that discipline cryptographic rather than rhetorical. The difficult part is no longer the signing primitive; it is keeping a large interlocking specification set from preserving two plausible versions of the same rule. The structural conflict checker is valuable precisely because human readers, including reviewers, repeatedly miss locally stale declarations even when the correction exists later in the same document.

The current v6 implementation has a sensible boundary: P1.1 owns exact bytes and local invariants, while project lifecycle and external referents remain for P1.2/P1.3. That boundary needs continued protection. It would be easy for a focused signature helper or temporary result type to acquire a success-sounding boolean and accidentally outrun what it verified.

## On the work done

The most useful part of this session was refusing to trust the initial green suite. Direct comparison against the frozen contract found several bugs that happy-path vectors did not expose: null workflow reconciliation, missing row hash acceptance, bare-key scheme handling, uncanonical classification, and the `1e21` contradiction. I am less satisfied with the size of `_verification.py`; P1.3 will have to integrate the v6 result model without creating a second verifier or preserving the focused P1.1 result as a competing authority.
